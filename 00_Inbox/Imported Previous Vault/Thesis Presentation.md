# Information Seeking, Consumption and Dissemination with LLM powered Information Systems
> Information is one of the most powerful forces in today’s digital era, shaping decisions at every level of society. While the ways we seek, disseminate and access information have evolved over time, core challenges—such as cultural barriers, echo chambers, misinformation, information overload, etc.—persist. With the advent of LLM-powered information systems, there is hope that capabilities like multilingual understanding, summarization, and their conversational nature can help overcome these long-standing issues. In this talk, we will examine the state of information flow with LLMs i.e. how humans are influenced by information from LLMs and how LLMs disseminate information in contested domains where there is no ground truth. We will summarize the core challenges that exist, and directions (upcoming works - if time permits) that can lead to more equitable information access, consumption, and dissemination.

>Hello everyone, thank you for inviting me. Let me first take a moment to introduce myself.
>I am Nikhil Sharma, a second year PhD in Computer Science at the Johns Hopkins University and aligned with the title of the talk I study information flows, their challenges and how to improve them. 
>George Miller in 1983 characterized humans as Informavore - beings that actively seek, consume and disseminate information.  I find this characterization both accurate and amusing. More importantly, I think we should emphasize what that creature does with that information, this creature most critically (that is us btw for any of you wondering), seeks information to find the truth, consumes information to update their beliefs, disseminates information to share their beliefs. This cycle is cycle of seeking, consuming and disseminating information is what I call information flows.
>It would not be an exaggeration to say that that these information flows shape us, what we believe in and infact even the reality of the world we live in.
>Historically, these information flows along with the systems enabling this have evolved. We first progressed from word of mouth, to news papers where the had greater access to information but the people who could disseminate were limited. This was followed by news broadcasting with television where we got an even bigger audience but again the dissemination power remained with a few people. But then we had a breakthrough with the web, the web gave us global access to consume information from around the world and in the early days of web it also gave everyone equal power to disseminate information. This shifted the power of narrative towards the user leading to more decentralized information flows.
