# Thesis Version — 2026-05-19

## One-Sentence Thesis
I study how conversational AI reshapes information flows and build theories, benchmarks, studies, and tools that help people move from abundant information to usable knowledge.

## Current Spine
1. Human behavior changes when AI becomes the interface for seeking, consuming, and disseminating information.
2. AI behavior in pluralistic contexts can amplify filter bubbles, narrative gaps, misinformation, and trust failures.
3. Better information futures require democratic, participatory, and infrastructural mechanisms: audits, feedback systems, information safety protocols, and research platforms.

## What Connects
The projects all touch the same substrate: information flows mediated by conversational AI. The relevant unit is not just the model output; it is the cycle of seeking, consumption, trust, feedback, dissemination, and governance.

## What Does Not Yet Connect
Mixed-initiative collaboration and speaker-centric preference data currently connect through communication and agency, but need a sharper bridge to information seeking and information governance.
