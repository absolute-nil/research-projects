# Compare Google, Social Media, Conversational Search, and Deep Search

## One-Line Summary
Comparative study of information-seeking platforms — Google, social media, conversational AI (ChatGPT-style), and deep search (Perplexity-style) — on interaction quality, polarization, misinformation, and information participation.

## Motivation
These platforms represent fundamentally different information architectures with different affordances, incentive structures, and effects on users. No comparative study has placed them in the same framework to measure their relative effects on information quality and healthy information behavior.

## Research Questions
| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| RQ1 | How does interaction quality (fluency, depth, satisfaction) differ across platforms? |
| RQ2 | How does each platform affect misinformation acceptance? |
| RQ3 | How does each platform affect polarization? |
| RQ4 | How does each platform enable or suppress "information participation" (the ability to contribute as well as consume)? |

## Variables
**Independent:** Platform type (Google / social media / conversational AI / deep search)
**Dependent:** Interaction quality, misinformation consumption, polarization change, information participation
**Moderators:** Topic type, user expertise, prior platform use

## Study Design
- **Type:** Between-subjects experiment with naturalistic tasks
- **Task:** Information-seeking on contested topics across platform conditions
- **Measures:** Pre/post belief scales, behavioral logs, user experience scales
